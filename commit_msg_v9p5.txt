V9-P5: gamma flip -- why the spiral stops

Part 8's honest boundary: "the kernel is still deterministic; it does
not yet let gamma evolve path-by-path." This module closes that gap.

Real crashes are self-amplifying but NOT self-sustaining: as implied
volatility spikes, dealer positions flip from short gamma (forced to
sell into the fall) to long gamma (buying dips becomes mechanically
attractive), and the spiral is cut. P5 adds one evolution rule to the
Part-5 kernel: once cumulative drawdown passes flip_threshold, gamma
converges toward +0.80 (deep long gamma) at flip_speed 0.60.

Results (defaults): no-flip reproduces the P1 baseline exactly
(-15.4%, 3.07x). With flip threshold 10%: -13.8% (2.76x), flip at
step 3, gamma ends +0.80. Threshold sweep: the later the flip, the
deeper the damage -- 5% -> 2.39x, 10% -> 2.76x, 15% -> 3.07x (too
late to matter), 20%+ -> never flips (3.07x). Monte Carlo (uncertain
gamma0/shock/threshold, 2000 paths): flip fires in 80% of paths;
p50 -14.0%, p10 -19.4%, p1 -23.7%, worst -27.2% -- vs no-flip p1
-28.6% and worst -33.4%. The flip truncates the tail: spiral cuts,
then price stabilizes (recovery itself is the intervention story).

Self-test: 7 invariants green (P1 reproduction, flip cuts the spiral,
gamma sign flip, threshold monotonicity, determinism, MC tail
shallower with flip, flip_share in (0,1)).
