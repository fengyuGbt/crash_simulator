feat(part-22): one override is all it takes — the self-exemption loophole (V10-P10)

Part 22 attacks Part 21's full commitment from the wearer side: the
exemption rate e in [0,1] — the probability that at the moment of
crisis the investor overrides the standing automatic rule despite full
commitment. e=0 reproduces Part 19's mechanical garment, e=1 reproduces
Part 20's naive investor.

Three findings:
1. Nonlinear damage: in the depleted world (C=0.25), a 10% exemption
   rate jumps the worst 1% from -22.0% to -56.2% — 85.5% of the
   garment's protection destroyed by one-tenth of a loophole. Extreme-
   order logic: as long as any override can fire, the deepest paths are
   exposed.
2. Violent asymmetry: commitment 0->0.75 bought 3.3 pts (Part 21);
   exemption 0->0.10 destroys 34.2 pts. Win needs 100% commitment,
   lose needs a single override (reliability cliff).
3. The loophole widens with capacity drain: destruction share 22.1%
   (C=0.75) -> 85.5% (C=0.25); absolute damage 6.7 -> 34.2 pts.

Anchors: Reinhart & Rogoff "This Time Is Different" (66 countries, 800
years; Templeton's four most expensive words); O'Donoghue & Rabin
present bias (AER 1999); Ashraf, Karlan & Yin SEED commitment savings
(QJE 2006): balances +81% (openers +337%) — the product worked because
exemption had friction.

Module: src/self_exemption.py, 250 lines, deterministic, stdlib only,
